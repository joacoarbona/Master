Section03Expert

Translation antipatterns — six failure modes the Senior Director must recognise and stop.

Each antipattern looks reasonable mid-flight. Each costs the function adoption velocity and credibility.

ANTIPATTERN 01
The technology-led solution
"We have an LLM, what should we do with it?" The business need is back-fit to the technology. Pilot succeeds, scale fails — because no business owner ever wanted the outcome.
ANTIPATTERN 02
The undefined success metric
"It will increase engagement." Engagement how, measured against what baseline, attributed how? The JD-named "clear metrics" requirement is precisely this antidote.
ANTIPATTERN 03
The orphan pilot
Pilot ships beautifully · no owner for scale · no operating model for adoption · no shut-off plan. The "one-off pilot" the JD warns against.
ANTIPATTERN 04
The "build new" reflex
Custom build when reuse from another unit exists. The JD's "build once, scale to many" exists precisely to interrupt this reflex.
ANTIPATTERN 05
The compliance afterthought
GDPR / AI Act / PV review happens at G3. Outcome: re-baselined plan · 6-month slip · sponsor confidence dented. Registry-before-scale exists to prevent this.
ANTIPATTERN 06
The "AI literacy" delegation
Change + literacy is delegated to HR/AITO without translator partnership. Result: training without context · adoption stalls · ROI claims unattributable.
"A translator's most valuable habit is not translation — it is refusal. Refusing the technology-led solution, the undefined metric, the orphan pilot. Refusal IS the work."
The Senior Director discipline
Build a translator's intake template that screens for these six antipatterns at G1. Any use case scoring two or more is sent back for re-shaping before resources are committed. The template IS the function's adoption insurance.
