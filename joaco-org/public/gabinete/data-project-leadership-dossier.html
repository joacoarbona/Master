Section04Expert

Federated vs centralised data architecture — pharma is consolidating around a hybrid model, and the Director's plans live or die by which mode the portfolio assumes.

Mismatching the delivery plan to the architecture mode is the #1 cause of cross-domain programme drift.

MODEL A · CENTRALISED

Single warehouse · platform-led

Data physically moves to a central platform · global standards enforced top-down.

Advantages
  • Single source of truth · easier reporting
  • Standardised tooling and access
  • Predictable cost per consumer
Director-level risks
  • Slow to onboard new domains
  • Local domain owners feel disempowered
  • Privacy / residency complexity at EU scale
MODEL B · FEDERATED / MESH

Data products · domain-led

Domains own their data as products · governance is federated · platform provides interoperability standards.

Advantages
  • Closer to domain reality · faster adoption
  • Privacy / residency naturally accommodated
  • Aligns with EHDS posture (Module IV)
Director-level risks
  • Higher coordination cost across products
  • Quality drift if standards not enforced
  • Demands strong product ownership culture
"Federate the data, centralise the standards" is the operating compromise most large pharmas are now arriving at. The Director's job is to deliver programmes that honour both at once.
Diagnostic question
In your first review of any portfolio item, ask: "Is this delivered in a centralised, federated, or hybrid mode — and does the operating model match?" Misalignment surfaces as scope drift in stage 3 and adoption failure in stage 4.

Continuous assurance

Quality, lineage, drift monitored continuously rather than at sample audit · monitoring dashboards co-owned by Automation and the programme.

PATTERN 03
DataOps

CI/CD applied to data pipelines · version control · test automation · environment promotion · roll-back discipline.

Director job: insist on DataOps maturity in the platform delivery plan — refuse delivery without it.
